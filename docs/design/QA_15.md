# 0.15 — Skill presentation and quality baseline

8 September 2026. This is an art/animation and verification pass over the 0.14 expedition, not another systems expansion. See [QUALITY_BAR.md](QUALITY_BAR.md) for the editable north star, current ratings, measurable proposed targets and owner-feedback column. The existing eight-stage systems and known scope limits remain in [QA_14.md](QA_14.md).

## What changed

- Returning blades now have a spinning steel silhouette and directional trails, with teal outbound/brass returning hubs. Planted plates are small beveled shards rather than plain marks.
- Gravity wells have curved intake arms and inward-traveling motes. Pull/push cones show opposing directional chevrons, with a strong initial read and fast fade. Guard sweep has a separate cutting edge.
- Core strike and Siege battery show descending steel shells and a countdown on their real damage boundary. Only center-critical strikes show the inner critical ring. Landing attacks retain their own convergence pattern.
- Rim cutter has a traveling steel/brass crescent over the actual outer payoff and inner boundary. Piston thrust has a shaft/head follow-through and extra rails on the empowered third cast.
- Nuke/rocket impacts use fast shock rings and restrained material shards without an opaque disc over enemies and projectiles. Movement gets directional follow-through; consume gets closing jaws.
- Bulkheads have braced rails and deploying end posts. The collision line is visible immediately; animation never delays collision feedback.
- Constructs unfold their tripods and show barrel recoil, pulse expansion, repair motes, opening mirror cores, hook direction or moving treads. Their lifetime arcs use the real upgraded lifetime; the former fixed-20-second assumption is removed.
- Ballast roll gets armor arcs and speed chevrons; repair gets brackets; veil leaves a quiet decoy silhouette. Three-contact progress has per-target pips.
- Icons separate previously near-identical movement, strike, melee and plate tools. A second small-size review led to another redesign of Core strike, Orbital entry, Siege battery, Bulkhead, Crosswire, Plate recall, Piston thrust and Scrap pursuit.
- Important cast/impact feedback can displace a cosmetic hit/pickup when the visual pool is full. Caps remain 180 normal/65 reduced. Reduced effects cuts secondary motifs and trails while retaining functional information.
- Added a repeatable skill-render fixture and the quality scorecard. No new menus, permanent progression system, generated bitmap pack or balance-number changes. Existing synthesized cut cues are retained through an event alias; this was not a new audio production pass.

All art is an extension of the existing code-native steel/teal/brass/cream family. No third-party art was copied and no runtime generation/API dependency was added. Simulation receives only presentation metadata/events; art does not spend combat RNG or change movement/hit geometry. Existing menu painting, user music and player saves remain untouched.

## Review and verification

The new `tests/skill_visual_test.gd` exercises 34 active catalog choices × three ranks (0/5/10) × two effects modes = 204 cases. Each runs 45 steps at 30 Hz, sampling five draw states. It checks successful casting, presentation/simulation separation, bounded event pools, priority displacement and expiry. Snapshot checks include actors, health/energy, projectiles, fields, blades, walls, constructs, plates, recasts and the three simulation RNG states.

The fixture passes **2,252 checks** headlessly and with actual OpenGL rendering on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070. The eight-skill showcase captures **360 frames**, plus a 64/32 px icon sheet, with **736 checks** and no logged script/drawing errors. Source fixtures are committed; captures and GIFs remain ignored under `output/`.

Visual iteration used baseline/after sheets, full-size stills and phase contact sheets. It caught weak cone contrast, repeated icon silhouettes, a summon hidden beneath a fixture enemy and a rolling fixture crashing too early. Those were corrected and captured again. The GPU UI run covered the loadout pages, milestones, equipment, mastery and combat: **3,877 visible labels, zero width overflows**. Width checks do not establish perfect usability or full-build readability.

The final `scripts/check.ps1` run passed **5,197 assertions**, the scene smoke test, and separate layout audits of **3,378 + 3,878 labels**, with no failures/width issues. It now includes the new visual fixture and rejects logged engine errors even when Godot exits 0. Its legacy 180-enemy/240-projectile stress fixture measured 12.37 ms mean / 27.98 ms maximum simulation tick; this is a remaining stress-budget concern, not a GPU frame-rate result.

Both legacy behavior probes were rerun with `-- --refined`. In the old three-round fixture, moving-passive policies lost all three sampled seeds; adaptive casting won two and adaptive casting plus commanded basics won three. These are diagnostics of a legacy fixture, not evidence that the current eight-stage campaign is balanced.

### A testing blind spot fixed

The expedition behavior probe previously seeded spawns/offers but not loot. That made historical runs incomparable even at the same displayed seed. It now seeds loot using the real controller convention (`run_seed + 901`); the art fixture does too. No game reward table was changed. Two independent probe executions now match all gameplay result fields, including casts/kills/level/time, for all six scenarios. Wall-clock performance samples are intentionally excluded from this equality comparison.

| Fixed-seed A0 scenario | Result | Time | Round reached |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gunner, idle | Lost | 18.1 s | 1 |
| Gunner, active bot | Lost | 194.3 s | 4 |
| Brawler, idle | Lost | 19.4 s | 1 |
| Brawler, active bot | Lost | 283.1 s | 5 |
| Engineer, idle | Lost | 18.1 s | 1 |

These show that standing still and this particular active policy differ; they do not prove satisfying difficulty, player win rates or class balance. The policy is not equally intelligent with every discovered ability. The short Engineer result is a playtest question, not enough evidence to buff a class automatically.

The corrected A5 Engineer **artificial-health** soak completed all 22 rounds: 1,801.4 simulated seconds, 4,968 kills, Power 91 and 114 peak enemies. On this execution, p95 simulation step was 614 microseconds; worst step 10,842 microseconds. This establishes route traversal and an observed simulation cost, **not normal-health balance or rendered FPS**. The machine used for rendered inspection has an RTX 5070; no minimum-spec claim is made.

## Reproduce the visual review

```powershell
.\.tools\godot\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe --path . --rendering-method gl_compatibility --audio-driver Dummy --script res://tests/skill_visual_test.gd
.\.tools\godot\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe --path . --rendering-method gl_compatibility --audio-driver Dummy --script res://tests/skill_visual_test.gd -- --showcase --folder=res://output/skill-review-final
```

The first command renders the 204 test cases and exits. The second saves the eight-skill sequence and `icons.png` without using player profiles or awarding rewards. The showcase uses controlled stationary enemies and deliberately staggered skills; it is not a crowd stress test. `output/skill-motion-review.gif` is the locally assembled review reel, not a shipped asset.

## Assessment and next work

The result is a more coherent **prototype**, not a finished public demo. Current estimates: controls/skills/art/readability/builds/rewards/UI/performance around 6/10; enemies/bosses and pacing around 5; environments 4; technical reliability 7. Audio remains unrated without a listening session. Detailed evidence and proposed acceptance exercises are in the quality bar; no score was raised merely because a test passed.

Outstanding: human control/impact feedback, dense-build VFX review, first-three-round balance and discovery pacing, recognizable boss identities, less repetitive environments, audio mix listening, a real GPU frame-time benchmark and a clean-machine Windows export. Forty equipment variants remain eight silhouettes in five treatments; eight bosses remain configurations of one body. This pass does not pretend to have produced unique art for all of them.

No user action was needed to implement this pass. The next useful input is a short playtest using the scorecard: weakest-feeling skill, most satisfying action, first moment of growth, an unclear damage event, and ratings you disagree with. Those answers should drive the next iteration ahead of adding more abilities.
